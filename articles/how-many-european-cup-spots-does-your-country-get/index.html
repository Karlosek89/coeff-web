How Many European Cup Spots Does Your Country Get?

7 September 2026 · Author: Karel Zbíral

The higher a country sits in the UEFA 5-year coefficient, the more clubs it sends into the Champions League, Europa League and Conference League next season — and the later (or never) those clubs have to go through qualifying. Here's the exact breakdown of where each rank lands.

This breakdown is officially called the access list, and UEFA rebuilds it every year from the current ranking of all 55 member countries. If you want to know how that ranking itself is calculated, we covered it in How Is the UEFA Country Coefficient Calculated? — this one picks up from there: how many spots, and which round.

Champions League

The most prestigious competition also has the steepest drop-off. The very top countries send four clubs straight into the 36-team league phase, while the bottom of the ranking has to win three qualifying rounds just to get there.

Conference League

The youngest and widest competition — and it's where the system's generosity really shows. It draws on four different sources depending on rank (cup winner, 2nd, 3rd and 6th place in the league), so the table is busier, but even the countries at the very bottom of the ranking get a representative in Europe.

Even the 55th-ranked country gets two representatives in the Conference League — they just have to clear one more round than clubs from the top ten.

What shifts a little every year

This breakdown is the base structure — it holds unless UEFA applies a one-off exception. Two of those happen routinely: a handful of positions get a so-called bye based on the club coefficient (not the country one), a one-off jump a round ahead, and there are two floating Champions League league-phase spots for the two countries with the best single-season coefficient (the European Performance Spot). Exactly what applies to your country this year is shown live in the Coeff app, in the Tickets section of the country detail.

In practice: Czechia currently holds 10th place — the last rank that still gives the champion a direct spot in the Champions League league phase, per the table above. Four spots higher, France's 5th place is under pressure from Portugal in 6th — and as the table shows, those two ranks carry a different starting round for their 4th-placed league team too, so a single spot in the ranking can be worth millions of euros. Rankings like these can move fast: see how Poland climbed from 28th to 11th in just a few years.
