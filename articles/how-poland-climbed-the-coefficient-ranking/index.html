How Poland Climbed From the UEFA Coefficient Basement to Knocking on the Top 10

7 August 2026

Poland's UEFA country coefficient ranking has climbed sharply over the past five years — from 28th place to 21st to 12th. Right now Poland sits 11th, just 2.5 points behind Czechia in the fight for a direct Champions League berth. That's one of the sharpest multi-year climbs in European football's ranking system.

The turning point lines up closely with the introduction of the UEFA Conference League in 2021/22. That competition handed lower-ranked associations like Poland extra entry spots that the Champions League and Europa League never offered. For the first time since 2002/03, four Ekstraklasa sides represented Poland in major European competition in a single season.

More clubs, more wins

The results followed. Polish clubs have recorded as many as 37 wins in European competition in a recent season — matching Italy and Belgium, and trailing only England's 41. That's a remarkable return for a country that was outside the top 30 not long ago.

It isn't just about one club, either. Legia Warszawa has been Poland's most consistent contributor over the last 15 years by a wide margin, but the last two seasons show the base widening: Raków Częstochowa went from a marginal contributor to Poland's top-scoring club in 2025/26, and Jagiellonia Białystok — long a minor name — suddenly added 20 points in 2024/25, its biggest single-season jump by any Polish club in over a decade. (The full 15-year, club-by-club breakdown is in a companion piece.)

Investment is following the results

Money is following the results, too — Widzew Łódź was bought by billionaire Robert Dobrzycki in spring 2025 and spent over €20 million in one winter transfer window alone, including record signing Osman Bukari.
